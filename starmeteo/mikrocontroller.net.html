The SPI communication with the RF receiver shows quite well what the weather station does after switching on. The device is a WD 4005, the receiver seems to be from AXSEM (see above) if it is an AX50424 I can not say but at least the registers for setting the frequency fit.

After inserting the batteries, the frequency of the outside temperature sensor is determined first, apparently there are three possible frequencies.

If the outside temperature sensor has been received or after a timeout, the frequency for the weather data is determined. For this purpose, the following frequencies are received:

1
 
466,230 MHz

2
 
466,075 MHz

3
 
465,970 MHz

Each frequency is in the range of approx. +/- 8 kHz with approx. 250 Hz steps go through. The whole thing is repeated a few times. This will probably compensate for tolerances of the quartz.

If the correct frequency has been detected (I do not yet know what the criterion is for it) only this frequency is received alternately with the outside temperature sensor.

Here (Region 81) the frequency for the weather data is 466,230 MHz.

At address 1208184, the region is transmitted every few minutes in a short message (two characters).

But there are also longer messages on this address, which could be information about when certain messages are sent. In the "WETTERdirekt 300" version you can see quite well that there must be information about the transmission time: When waiting for the weather, the receiver is first switched on every 10 minutes for 90 seconds. Whether the receiver is switched on is clearly visible by the current consumption, which is then significantly higher at about 29 mA (otherwise below 1 mA).

David G. wrote:

Can you log in if the outdoor thermometer is being paired?

I have already briefly described this above. After the battery has been inserted, a signal from the outside temperature sensor is waited for at three frequencies, if none is received, then the outside temperature sensor will no longer be taken into account later.

When a signal has been received, it is regularly received at the corresponding frequency.

Presumably, the reception of the outside temperature is completely set when the signal is no longer coming, which makes sense because the power consumption is very high when the receiver is switched on.
